# What do existing trek and expedition planning apps offer?

Research for [issue #8](https://github.com/ChrisHorrocks/gata/issues/8), written on 12 November 2026 (Europe/London). It is meant to inform the gata v1 spec: a planner for small teams on multi-day treks with camping.

## Method

Claims below are taken from each product's own website, feature pages, pricing or plan pages, help centre, training docs, or official news. I did not rely on third-party listicles or reviews. Two official pages blocked or rendered as empty JavaScript shells in this environment:

- [alltrails.com/plans](https://www.alltrails.com/plans) and [alltrails.com/welcome](https://www.alltrails.com/welcome) returned HTTP 403, so AllTrails list prices are cited from [AllTrails Help: the REI partnership](https://support.alltrails.com/hc/en-us/articles/53448489243668-The-AllTrails-partnership-with-REI), which states the standard annual prices.
- [lighterpack.com](https://lighterpack.com/) is a client-rendered app. Product behaviour is cited from that official web app (including its first-party UI copy) and from [LighterPack's privacy policy](https://lighterpack.com/privacy).
- [caltopo.com/subscription/update](https://caltopo.com/subscription/update) did not return a usable public feature matrix (it appears to require a signed-in account). CalTopo prices and the Free / Mobile / Pro split are cited from the official plans/FAQ page at [caltopo.com/northern](https://caltopo.com/northern).

Paywalled features are marked in the table and called out in the product notes. "Not found" means I did not find the feature on official pages, not that a hidden capability is impossible.

The named apps in the ticket are all included. For group trip planners I added **Hikeset** and **Pack Hiker**, the two official products I found that most closely combine a shared multi-day itinerary with group gear coordination.

## Product sketches

### Gaia GPS

A general backcountry navigation and route-planning app (web and mobile) with a large map catalogue. A free account can drop waypoints, record tracks, follow the current location, back up data, share tracks, and print Gaia Topo maps. Offline map downloads, the full catalogue (the help centre says over 250 maps and overlays; the marketing homepage says 300+ layers), layered overlays, and printing of all maps are Premium. Official pages describe route planning with snap-to-trail or straight-line modes, elevation profiles, weather / wildfire / avalanche and terrain overlays (slope angle, snow depth, cell coverage), and GPX / KML / GeoJSON / FIT import plus GPX / KML / GeoJSON export. Groups can share a folder and grant view or edit access, with real-time updates while connected. Official membership docs describe Free, Premium, and Premium with Outside+ tiers but do not list a current dollar price on the pages I opened. I found no kit lists, meal planning, or trip-level membership model.

Sources: [membership options](https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003524547-Membership-Options-Free-Premium-and-Premium-with-Outside), [homepage](https://www.gaiagps.com/), [offline help](https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003639448-Does-Gaia-GPS-work-offline), [create and measure routes](https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003640568-Create-and-Measure-Routes-on-gaiagps-com), [import](https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/360052763513-Import-GPX-KML-KMZ-GeoJSON-or-FIT-Files-on-gaiagps-com), [export](https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003524687-Export-Data-as-GPX-KML-or-GeoJSON-from-gaiagps-com), [printing](https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003526147-Printing-on-gaiagps-com), [shared folders](https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000917048-Using-Shared-Folders-on-gaiagps-com).

### FarOut

A trail- and river-guide navigation app (formerly a Pacific Crest Trail guide) with 250+ handcrafted guides. You buy a single guide as a lifetime purchase, or subscribe to FarOut Unlimited (US$15 / month, US$72 / six months, or US$96 / year; annual includes 100 points toward lifetime guide purchases). After one download, the guide works fully offline: live GPS location, elevation profile, water / campsite / junction waypoints with detail pages and photos, town and resupply guides, community waypoint comments, check-ins for friends and family, and a custom route-building tool for planning a day's mileage, ascent and descent. Official pages do not describe shared kit lists, meals, or a team trip object.

Sources: [features](https://faroutguides.com/features/), [homepage](https://faroutguides.com/), [subscription](https://faroutguides.com/subscription/), [custom routes in the official feature article](https://faroutguides.com/5-next-level-farout-features-you-should-know-for-your-thru-hike/).

### AllTrails

A trail-discovery and navigation product with a large community catalogue. Free members can save trails, create lists, and navigate. **Plus** (standard price US$35.99 / year on official Help) adds offline trail, park and custom-area downloads, 3D maps, printed maps, trail conditions (temperature, precipitation, snow depth, air quality, mosquitoes), community heatmaps, wrong-turn alerts, live activity sharing, Garmin / COROS send, Elevation Mode, and Trip Assistant, which generates a day-by-day itinerary of *recommended trails* (a tour of day hikes, not a single backpacking route split into camps). **Peak** (standard price US$79.99 / year) adds advanced map layers, the advanced custom route builder (including on mobile), and Route Assistant / multi-day backpacking tools that split a long route into daily segments by distance, elevation and points of interest, with waypoints for camps, water, resupply and bail-outs. File upload (including GPX tracks, routes and waypoints) is available to members. Free members can export their own activity recordings or custom routes in many formats including GPX; exporting a verified trail page is a Plus or Peak feature. I found no kit lists or meal planning.

Sources: [premium benefits](https://support.alltrails.com/hc/en-us/articles/43589223013652-The-benefits-of-AllTrails-premium-membership), [REI partnership pricing](https://support.alltrails.com/hc/en-us/articles/53448489243668-The-AllTrails-partnership-with-REI), [multi-day backpacking](https://support.alltrails.com/hc/en-us/articles/50326663440020-Planning-a-multi-day-backpacking-route-with-AllTrails), [uploading files](https://support.alltrails.com/hc/en-us/articles/37228498475028-Uploading-files-to-AllTrails), [downloading files](https://support.alltrails.com/hc/en-us/articles/37230403315476-Downloading-files-from-AllTrails), [Navigate overview](https://support.alltrails.com/hc/en-us/articles/360059000272-Navigate-feature-overview).

### Komoot

A route planner and navigator used heavily for hiking and cycling. Anyone can plan routes worldwide; navigation and recording require an unlocked region (one single region is free for first-time users). Regions are a one-time purchase (official product page: US$3.99 for a single region, US$8.99 for a region bundle) and include turn-by-turn voice navigation, offline maps or routes, lifelong updates, and GPX download. **Premium** (a subscription; I did not find a list price on the official Premium page I opened) adds worldwide maps and navigation, multi-day routes, Personal Collections, Live Tracking, weather on route, sport-specific maps, 3D maps, and the komoot map on Garmin devices. The multi-day planner splits one continuous route of about seven hours or more into daily stages, with accommodation as end points, and saves the trip as a Personal Collection. Route planning and editing, auto-rerouting, and Live Tracking need a connection; downloaded routes and regional maps work offline. I found no kit lists or meal planning, and no small-team trip membership model.

Sources: [product / Maps pricing](https://www.komoot.com/product), [Maps vs Premium](https://support.komoot.com/hc/en-us/articles/10163258809626-komoot-plans-Maps-and-Premium), [Premium features](https://www.komoot.com/premium), [multi-day routes](https://support.komoot.com/hc/en-us/articles/10269672250266-Plan-multi-day-routes), [offline use](https://support.komoot.com/hc/en-us/articles/10356476920986-Download-routes-and-maps-for-offline-use).

### OS Maps

Ordnance Survey's official UK mapping and navigation app (also web, tablet and smartwatch), with topographic maps, aerial imagery and street mapping. Free: OS Standard Mapping, plot / record / follow routes, and thousands of partner routes. **Premium** is £7.49 / month or £35.99 / year on the official discover page, and unlocks offline map and route downloads, all 607 OS Leisure maps, topographic maps for the USA, New Zealand and Australia, global snap-to-path, and unlimited map printing on the web. Official guides describe creating routes, distance and elevation, sharing routes, and GPX import; "every route created on the OS Maps App has a downloadable GPX file available for premium members." Community Alerts let users report passable or impassable hazards (flooding, livestock, blocked paths, vegetation) that appear as live map icons. Official walking-route guidance tells people to check a weather forecast *outside* the app; I did not find a built-in weather overlay on the official pages I opened. I found no kit lists, meal planning, or a team trip object.

Sources: [OS Maps discover / plan comparison](https://osmaps.com/discover/osmaps/), [how to plan a walking route](https://osmaps.com/discover/guides/how-to-plan-a-walking-route-using-os-maps/), [GPX FAQs](https://osmaps.com/discover/guides/gpx-file-faqs-a-complete-guide-for-hikers/), [Community Alerts announcement](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/news/hazard-reporting-tool-introduced-for-os-maps).

### CalTopo

A backcountry mapping platform (web and mobile) aimed at serious trip planning and also used by search-and-rescue. Official copy emphasises collaborative map building, stackable layers, slope-angle shading, SNOTEL snow depth, daily and weekly satellite imagery, stream gauges, elevation-specific wind and weather, and public / private land. Multiple users can edit the same map at the same time, with edits appearing in real time; shareable links grant Read, Update (add only) or Write access. Offline base-layer downloads in the mobile app need at least a Mobile subscription. Official FAQ: a Free account can create and share maps; Pro is US$50 / year and adds offline maps, 3D, weekly satellite imagery and more; Mobile is US$20 / year if you only want offline maps. New subscribers can try Pro for 14 days with no credit card. Units are switchable in settings, including metric. I found no kit lists or meal planning; "collaboration" here means shared *maps*, not a trip with roles, meals or pack lists.

Sources: [about](https://caltopo.com/about), [subscription plans / FAQ](https://caltopo.com/northern), [account and subscription](https://training.caltopo.com/all_users/accounts), [FAQs](https://training.caltopo.com/all_users/getting-started/faq), [sharing and collaboration](https://training.caltopo.com/all_users/share), [map sharing permissions](https://training.caltopo.com/all_users/share/permissions).

### LighterPack

A free, open-source website for personal packing lists. Official copy: enter packing lists, visualise pack weights, and share lists with others. The official app has a reusable **gear library** ("Items you add to a list show up here"; items can be dragged into a list). Lists are organised in categories; items have weights, optional prices, quantities, and worn / consumable flags. Totals break down into consumable weight, worn weight, base weight, and total pack weight. Units are per-item (the official app uses an author unit such as ounces or grams) with a list-level display unit. Sharing gives the list a public URL; anyone with the link can view it, embed it, or export it as CSV, with no account required to view. CSV import is built in. Official pages do not describe maps, itineraries, meals as a trip plan, accounts-per-trip collaboration, or assigning a shared item to one carrier on a team trip. Linked items can appear in more than one of *your* lists; that is not group coordination.

Sources: [lighterpack.com](https://lighterpack.com/) (official web app copy), [privacy policy](https://lighterpack.com/privacy) (product description, share-link and CSV behaviour).

### Hikeset

A group backpacking planner: routes, a day-by-day itinerary, camps, and coordinated gear. You can start a trip by uploading a GPX or KML file or by copying an existing trail, place camps for a daily mileage breakdown, and invite members by email. The organiser sets gear requirements ("everyone needs one" or "group needs X total"). Members link a personal pack list; a live dashboard shows what is covered (green) or still missing (red), including who is sharing with whom. Personal pack lists track weight in pounds or grams, with base / worn / consumable-style views and custom fields (the help text gives cost as an example). The free **Hiker** plan is designed for most trips (5 members per trip, 10 community members, gear coordination and route maps). **Explorer** and **Guide** are "by request" and add higher member limits and "upload custom GPX trails" — the help article also describes GPX / KML upload as a way to create a trip, so the exact free-tier upload limit is slightly ambiguous on official pages. Official help describes copying someone else's trip as a template. I found no meal planning, weather, offline maps, or live location sharing.

Sources: [homepage](https://hikeset.com/), [plans](https://hikeset.com/a/plans/), [help](https://hikeset.com/a/help/).

### Pack Hiker

A shared planner for multi-day hikes, covering itinerary, routes and maps, food, gear, expenses and safety, on the web and offline on Android. Official help (updated November 2026) describes: GPX / KML / GeoJSON upload with computed distance and ascent; optional in-app route drawing (Pro); a day-by-day itinerary of camps, travel, activities and routes, including rest ("zero") days; backpack weight per day from base weight + water + remaining food; personal meal plans as breakfast / lunch / dinner / snacks with item weights, or coarser daily / trip totals; shared food drops with packing lists; a *shared checklist* gear list (owner / co-admin managed) where each member ticks packing status, plus a personal base-weight number rather than a weighed item library rolled into the trip; expenses and settle-up; emergency contacts, PLBs, a safety brief and a post-hike check-in that emails contacts if you miss the deadline; invite-by-email with roles (view only, full edit, co-admin, owner); and a server-generated PDF export of the plan (Pro). During beta every account has Pro; after beta, Free is one active hike you own and up to four members, and PDF export stays Pro. Units are per-person (miles and pounds, or kilometres and kilograms). Official help states maps are planning tools and that you should still carry a dedicated navigation app or paper map.

Sources: [homepage](https://packhiker.com/), [getting started](https://packhiker.com/help/getting-started), [itinerary](https://packhiker.com/help/itinerary), [routes and maps](https://packhiker.com/help/routes-maps), [food](https://packhiker.com/help/food-planning), [gear](https://packhiker.com/help/gear), [members and roles](https://packhiker.com/help/members-permissions), [offline](https://packhiker.com/help/offline-mode), [PDF export](https://packhiker.com/help/pdf-export), [account, plans and units](https://packhiker.com/help/account-privacy).

## Table stakes versus differentiators

### Table stakes for a *mapping and navigation* app

Across Gaia, FarOut, AllTrails, Komoot, OS Maps and CalTopo, the common core is:

1. A map with topographic detail, usually with satellite or aerial imagery available.
2. The ability to create or import a route, see distance and elevation, and place waypoints.
3. GPX (or equivalent) interchange with other apps and GPS devices.
4. Offline maps and GPS location for use without signal — almost always paywalled, but treated as a core paid feature rather than a novelty.
5. Some way to share a route or map with other people (a link, a folder, a collection).

Weather overlays, 3D terrain, public-land boundaries, heatmaps, and turn-by-turn voice nav are widespread paid add-ons, not a guarantee on the free tier.

### Table stakes for a *kit-weight* tool

LighterPack defines this niche: a reusable personal gear library, categories, item weights, worn / consumable / base / pack totals, and an easy share link. Hikeset reuses that personal-list idea inside a trip. The big mapping apps do not offer this at all.

### Table stakes for a *small-team trek planner*

Only Hikeset and Pack Hiker, in this set, treat "the trip" as the product: a roster, invites, a day-by-day itinerary with camps, and some form of group gear coordination. Pack Hiker also treats meals, a printable plan, and a safety brief as part of that core. Mapping apps stop at the line and the waypoints.

### Differentiators

- **FarOut** is differentiated by expert, trail-specific guides (water, camps, resupply, town info) plus a live comment layer, not by general-purpose mapping or group logistics.
- **CalTopo** is differentiated by serious GIS-style layers (slope, snow, satellite, land status, weather) and true real-time multi-editor maps. That is map collaboration, not trip logistics.
- **OS Maps** is differentiated, for UK users, by official Ordnance Survey leisure mapping, public rights of way, and Community Alerts.
- **AllTrails** is differentiated by the size of its community trail catalogue, plus Peak's automatic day-splitting for backpacking routes.
- **Komoot** is differentiated by polished multi-day stage planning (including suggested stays) and sport-specific routing, especially for hiking and cycling.
- **Gaia GPS** is differentiated by the breadth of its map catalogue and terrain / safety overlays, plus simple shared folders for group map data.
- **LighterPack** is differentiated by being a specialist, free, item-level pack-weight tool with a reusable library — and by *not* being a trip planner.
- **Hikeset** is differentiated by a coverage dashboard for group gear (what is still missing, who is sharing with whom) on top of a camp-by-camp itinerary.
- **Pack Hiker** is differentiated by covering the whole trip logistics surface in one product: itinerary, meals and food drops, expenses, safety check-in, offline Android use, and a PDF pack-out.

## Gaps a small-team trek planner like gata could fill

The market is split. The established mapping apps are strong at routes, maps, offline use, weather and community data, and weak at the things gata has already put in v1: a shared trip of days and camps, a reusable personal gear library, shared items with one carrier, pack-weight totals, and per-day meals. LighterPack is strong at item weights and weak at trips and maps. That split is the opening.

It is not an empty opening. **Hikeset** already offers a group itinerary with camps, email invites, and coordinated gear. **Pack Hiker** already covers almost all of the agreed gata v1 surface — GPX on an OSM-based map, day-by-day camps, rest days, meals with weights, pack-weight totals, an organiser and editable membership, a printable plan and GPX re-export — and then goes further into expenses, food drops, emergency contacts and offline Android use. Any honest gap analysis has to treat Pack Hiker as the closest like-for-like product, not as a footnote.

Where gata can still be distinct, based on this evidence:

1. **Item-level pack weight that follows people onto a trip.** LighterPack has the library and the totals, but no trip. Hikeset links personal lists to a coverage dashboard, which answers "is a stove on the trip?" more directly than it answers "what does Alice's pack weigh tomorrow, including the tent and her dinners?" Pack Hiker's gear tab is a shared *checklist* plus a single base-weight number. gata's agreed model — a reusable personal library with gram weights, shared items assigned to one carrier, and meal weights (and kilocalories) counting toward each person's pack — is tighter than either group planner's official gear model, and it is something the mapping apps do not attempt.

2. **Meals as a first-class, shared part of the pack plan.** Official pages for the mapping apps, LighterPack and Hikeset do not offer meal planning. Pack Hiker does, but meals are personal (with an optional "follow the pack's food-drop tags" helper). I did not find a calorie field, or a "this dinner is carried by X" assignment, in Pack Hiker's official food docs. If gata ships breakfast / lunch / dinner / snacks with weight, kilocalories and a carrier, that is a real difference, not a me-too feature.

3. **A planning tool that does not pretend to be a navigator.** Pack Hiker already says this out loud: its maps are for planning, and you should still carry a dedicated nav app or a paper map. That is a coherent stance for gata's import-GPX-and-export-a-trip-sheet v1, and it matches how people already use Gaia, CalTopo, OS Maps, AllTrails or FarOut to draw the line and then need somewhere else to plan the *team* side. The practical gap, then, is **high-quality import**: taking a GPX (and its waypoints) from those tools and turning it into days, camps and a clean export, rather than rebuilding a route editor.

4. **A UK- and Europe-shaped product.** OS Maps is the official UK navigator but has no team logistics. Pack Hiker's official map docs lean on New Zealand and Queensland layers; Hikeset's public guides include US trails such as the John Muir Trail. A small-team trek planner that works naturally with OS / European routes, metric units, and a printable trip sheet is less crowded than "another US trail app."

5. **Self-serve, small-group software without a social network.** AllTrails, Komoot, FarOut and OS Maps invest in community content. gata does not need that to be useful. Hikeset and Pack Hiker already point in this "private group plan" direction; the opportunity is to do the kit-and-meals core more carefully than to out-community the map platforms.

## Out-of-scope items the evidence might pull into v1

These are currently out of scope for gata v1. The evidence below is only a flag; it does not decide the scope.

- **Offline use on the trail.** For every mapping / navigation product in this set, offline maps are table stakes on the paid tier. Pack Hiker also treats an offline copy of the *plan* (and Android topo along the route) as part of the product. gata's agreed substitute — a print-friendly trip sheet and a GPX with camps as waypoints — is a recognised pattern (Gaia, AllTrails, OS Maps and Pack Hiker all push a printed or PDF backup). The evidence still says people expect to take the plan into places with no signal. If v1 stays online-only, the export and print-out need to be good enough to stand in for offline, or this may come back early.

- **Safety plans and emergency contacts.** Mapping apps routinely frame "share your route" or live location (CalTopo locators and live tracks, Komoot Live Tracking, AllTrails Live Share, FarOut check-ins, OS Maps "share your route and expected return time") as safety. Pack Hiker puts emergency contacts, PLBs, a safety brief and a missed-check-in email in the core planner, and includes them in the PDF. A thin version — contacts and a route summary on the trip sheet, without a full safety-plan product — is the piece of this cluster that official sources most consistently treat as part of trip planning rather than a later extra.

- **Imperial units as a per-person setting.** LighterPack, Hikeset, Pack Hiker, CalTopo and Gaia all support or default to mixed or imperial units. Metric-only is reasonable for a UK-first v1, but per-person units are normal in this category, especially if any users plan in official US tools.

- **Copying an old trip as a template.** Hikeset documents this directly. Komoot's multi-day flow saves a Collection of stages that can be reused as saved routes. AllTrails lets you customise an existing route as the base of a trip. This is a convenience, not a foundation, but official docs treat it as an obvious way to start the next plan.

- **In-app route drawing.** This *is* table stakes for mapping apps, and it is paywalled at the "save" or "advanced" layer in Gaia and AllTrails. It is *not* table stakes for the group planners: both Hikeset and Pack Hiker are built around importing a track (Pack Hiker's drawing tools are a Pro extra). gata's import-only v1 matches that second group, as long as import and waypoint-to-camp mapping are solid.

- **Live collaborative editing.** CalTopo differentiates on this; Gaia shared folders update in real time while connected. Hikeset's gear dashboard is described as live. The mapping-app version is "many people on one map," not "many people on one itinerary, kit list and meal plan." Versioned saves with a conflict warning, as already agreed, are consistent with Pack Hiker's official stance that shared structural edits wait for a connection and that meal edits surface a conflict prompt. Nothing here forces live editing into v1.

- **Costs and cost splitting, ingredient-level shopping lists, and permits.** Among these products, only Pack Hiker has a first-class expenses feature, and only Pack Hiker has food-drop packing lists (a shopping / packing worksheet for resupply boxes, not a grocery builder). I did not find a permit workflow on official pages. These still look like later-version features, not holes that would make a v1 itinerary-kit-meals planner feel incomplete relative to the category.
